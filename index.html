About

In front of Mt Cook (Aoraki), Aoraki / Mount Cook National Park, New Zealand.

I'm a Fellow Postdoctoral Researcher at Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory, working at A-Lab, an autonomous laboratory for materials discovery. I build systems that let language models guide real experiments, which means most of my work comes back to one question: when can you trust what a model decided? That question is what's pulling me toward AI safety and evaluations. I'm currently building an evaluation framework focused on model calibration and sycophancy, measuring not just whether a model is right, but whether it knows when it isn't.

I started my scientific journey studying physics at the Aristotle University of Thessaloniki, then moved to Annecy, France for a PhD in experimental particle physics at CERN. If you'd like to know more, you can find my PhD thesis here. After that I moved into data science, applying the same core mindset to different problems: combine theory with data to make sense of complex systems.

I revel in the problem-solving side, but I don't want to stop there. Being able to communicate findings and their theoretical underpinnings to audiences of wildly different backgrounds has always been close to my heart.

When I'm not sifting through bits and bytes, you'll often find me running or cycling in the mountains. Apart from a slight obsession with challenges of all kinds, I just love being outdoors. And then there's painting, my preferred form of meditation. Have a look here if you're curious.
